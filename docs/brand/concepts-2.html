[brand sheet] round 2 · for review

The seat is hot. It's cooling.

A second concept with no king, hill, crown or throne anywhere in it. I read "not using the king of the hill stuff" as dropping the royalty idea altogether, name included, so this one starts again from what the product does. If you only meant the KOTH name, say so and I'll adjust.

The idea

One seat. It pays whoever is sitting in it. The moment you sit down it is hot, and it cools every second until someone decides it is cheap enough to take from you.

"The hot seat" is already how people describe a spot everyone is watching and nobody keeps for long. Heat also happens to fit every part of the mechanism, which is why I think it can carry the whole brand without help:

Price right after a takeoverHot. The seat was just bought at a premium.
The meltCooling. 2 to 8% a minute, shown as the seat losing its colour.
Trading volumeWhat keeps it warm. Rising volume, slower cooling. That is the real rule in the program.
The floorRoom temperature. Fees keep coming in, so it never goes fully cold.
The 10% burnAlready called a burn. Every takeover puts heat back in.
A takeoverYou are unseated, and paid for it.

The mark

A lowercase h is already a chair seen from the side: a back, a seat, two legs. So the name's first letter is the logo, and nothing has to be added to it.

otseat

The three dashes are heat coming off the seat. They are dropped at 24px and below. The wordmark is Inter with the chair standing in for the h; a final version would be drawn and spaced by hand.

h
The letter as typed.
Squared off: back, seat, legs. Still reads as h.
Heat marks added. They double as the gauge.
  • Why it is ours. The mark is the name, and its colour is the product's state. It could not be put on another project without both stopping making sense.
  • Weakness. An h-as-chair is a neat trick other people have found before, in furniture branding especially. Here the heat behaviour is what makes it specific, so the static mark is the less distinctive half.
  • Also weaker than round 1 on one point. A crown says "prize" at a glance. A chair needs the word next to it, or the colour moving, before it says anything.

One thing flips from the current design notes: red used to mean "dying, about to be taken". Here the hot colour means the opposite, freshly taken and expensive. The melt bar and the seat always share a colour, so the meaning stays in one place.

Two other roads I looked at and left

  • Toll. Creator fees are a toll on every trade, and one person holds the booth. True to where the money comes from, but it says nothing about the price falling, and a barrier arm coming down reads as "closed".
  • Melt. A block of ice you hold while it shrinks. The most literal option and a good word, but ice has no reason to pay you, so the earning half of the product goes unexplained.

Against round 1, and what to check

  • Round 1 (pixel crown) is the easier sell on day one: everyone gets "take the crown" instantly, and it fits the 2024 look. It also carries the pump.fun association with it.
  • Round 2 (hotseat) stands on its own, explains more of the mechanism, and has the cleaner logo. It asks people to learn one new word for the thing they are buying.
  • My pick is hotseat if the project should outlive one coin or one platform, and the pixel crown if it is a single launch aimed at people who remember that era.
  • Nothing is cleared. "Hot seat" is a common phrase and there are certain to be existing products called Hotseat. I have not checked trademarks, domains or handles for it.
  • Renaming has a cost. The repo, the program and the docs say "crown" throughout. The on-chain program would keep its names; the site, announcer and docs would change.
  • Still a sketch. Drawn in code to judge the idea. The chosen mark would be redrawn and exported properly.
